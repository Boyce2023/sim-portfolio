                                                  Market Commentary - so much happened, but not much changed

                                                                      All of the stock gains we saw in August (for the S&P 500) occurred in the first 2 days of
                                                                      trading — in the wake of results from a slew of Mega-cap Tech stocks that continued to
                                                                      deliver what the market has priced in. But following those early August gains, as the
                                                                      calendar shifted to deep summer, and catalysts gave way to vacation plans, risk assets
                                                                      have struggled to find a direction — even with the positive catalyst of strong results from
                                                                      NVDA last week. Yields on 10-year Treasuries — the focal point of much trepidation
                                                                      from both investors and policymakers — are up a bit from where they started the month
                                                                      (9bp), but not by much. Similarly, front-month Brent oil is trading up only marginally
                                                                      from where it was on July 31st. And the biggest moves have come in assets that have
                                                                      little-to-no fundamental value: gold, silver and bitcoin, up 9%, 22% and 24%,
                                                                      respectively.

                                                                      As Ben Snider illustrated in the Aug-21 Kickstart, “Hedge funds and mutual funds differ
                                                                      on AI but converge on Financials,” the combination of strong earnings and a largely
                                                                      range-bound S&P 500 has actually driven DOWN valuation recently. The S&P 500 is now




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                      trading at a NTM P/E multiple of 20X — elevated versus long-term history, but in-line
                                                                      with much of the post-pandemic echo-boom history.

                                                                      And against this market backdrop, we are focused on a slew of fundamental
                                                                      developments that may set a different path for stocks through the remainder of the year,
                                                                      including earnings, the Fed, Treasury, the Strait, AI (of course), and a whole lot of data.

                                                                      2Q earnings season was extraordinary. The median stock in the S&P 500 posted 14%
                                                                      EPS growth in 2Q26 highlighting the breadth and depth of the fundamental
                                                                      underpinnings of this sustained post-pandemic echo-boom rally (see Ben Snider’s
                                                                      Aug-14 Kickstart, “What Q2 earnings reports signaled about the state of corporate AI
                                                                      adoption”). Consequently, even though we entered the year trading at a 20X P/E on our
                                                                      next 12 months EPS forecast, and the S&P 500 is up 12% ytd, the market is still trading
                                                                      at a 20X P/E today — i.e., earnings is driving stocks, not valuation. CL stocks where are
                                                                      analysts are meaningfully above consensus: TPG, VRTX, LOAR, and CLS.

                                                                      The Fed pivoted without pivoting. The Fed made no changes to the Fed funds rate
                                                                      during the July meeting but in a speech at the Fed’s annual symposium last week, Fed
                                                                      Chair Warsh set a hawkish tone (see “USA: In Hawkish Jackson Hole Debut, Chairman
                                                                      Warsh Says the Fed’s ‘Predominant Focus Right Now Should Be On Prices’”). Warsh
                                                                      characterized the inflation numbers as ‘concerning’ and added that ‘we must be
                                                                      confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient
                                                                      speed… otherwise, we have work to do.’ A September hike is now possible if CPI and PCE
                                                                      come in firmer — but we don’t expect that either will. Finally, on the Fed, Allison Nathan
                                                                      explored what reduced transparency from the central bank may mean for markets in the
                                                                      Aug-19 Top of Mind note, “Assessing a less transparent Fed”). Our economists believe
                                                                      that reduced transparency leads to increased market volatility.

                                                                      The Treasury twisted. Treasury announced in August that it will increase the amount of
                                                                      government bonds it repurchases each month in an effort to suppress long-term yields.
                                                                      But Will Marshall writes that while Treasury’s liquidity support buyback operations
                                                                      should help market technicals at the margin, it is unlikely to drive a major decline in
                                                                      yields in “Q&A on Treasury’s Long-End Buybacks.”

                                                                     months of one of the world’s most important energy trade routes being ostensibly

                                                                     alleviating future pressure on oil prices in “Oil: How Much Oil Is the Gulf Exporting?”

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile

                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
                                                  For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

                                                  companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2
                                                  representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard
                                                  departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not
