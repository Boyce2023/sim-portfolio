                                                                                             We benchmark the beginning of the cloud security cycle to 2015: this is when
                                                                                             Amazon first disclosed AWS metrics at $4.6bn in 2014 revenue and 17% operating
                                                                                             margin, in contrast to the consensus view that cloud was a low or negative margin
                                                                                             business. Through 2016 and 2017, our industry conversations suggested that the cloud
                                                                                             security ecosystem was unchartered and somewhat chaotic territory: there was a fair
                                                                                             amount of confusion amongst CISOs as to how to secure cloud workloads, there were
                                                                                             many new point products to consider, and the hyperscalers began to embed security
                                                                                             functionality into their PaaS offerings as a means of differentiation. In early cloud years,
                                                                                             we believe many CISOs simply routed traffic back to their existing firewall DMZ to apply
                                                                                             security policies, with minimal new demand for cloud-specific security products. Early
                                                                                             cloud workloads also tended to be customer facing, which generally required less
                                                                                             security than internal facing workloads (e.g. content streaming from Netflix and
                                                                                             eCommerce websites did not require complex network and endpoint tooling).




                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                                             There were milestones in 2016, 2017, and 2018 that illustrated a maturation in the
                                                                                             Cloud Security Market. Several market dynamics began to converge: CISOs had a better
                                                                                             understanding of what security building blocks were needed in the cloud, point products
                                                                                             began to mature in the quality of their offerings to justify incremental spend beyond
                                                                                             hyperscaler security, customers began to consider more regulated and sensitive
                                                                                             workloads in the cloud, and multicloud architectures became more common. In parallel:

                                                                                             n     Gartner first published its Cloud Access Security Broker (CASB) Magic Quadrant in
                                                                                                   late 2017. CASBs delivered a layer of security between enterprise users and SaaS
                                                                                                   hosted applications like Salesforce.
                                                                                             n     2018 was the first big year of Security Cloud M&A, with Palo Alto acquiring
                                                                                                   Evident.io for ~$200mn and RedLock for $173mn, and Check Point acquiring Dome9
                                                                                                   for $175mn. In 2019, Palo Alto acquired Twistlock for $410M, PureSec for $47M and
                                                                                                   Aporeto for $150M in 2019. There was also roughly $1.3B of M&A before cloud

                                                  Why new AI security budget disproportionately accrues to today’s platforms

                                                                         The biggest risk in Software is that new AI value accrues to new entrants (AI natives,
                                                                         frontier models), rather than incumbents. This risk is much lower in Security for two
                                                                         reasons: 1) Security roadmaps are driven by ML innovation, not GenAI innovation;
                                                                         and this fundamental difference makes it hard to replicate the advantages of deep
                                                                         human reinforcement learning feedback loops and labeled datasets; 2) Security
                                                                         leaders are aggressive in acquiring new technology assets from a richly-funded
                                                                         private company landscape.


                                                                         1. Security roadmaps are driven by ML innovation, not GenAI innovation.
                                                                         We believe there is a structural difference in the type of AI that drives Security
                                                                         roadmaps vs. the type of AI that is driving frontier lab roadmaps that creates two
                                                                         distinct swim lanes that will likely not cross. Security companies have discussed
                                                                         Machine Learning (ML) as being the cornerstone of their R&D roadmaps for 10+ years.
                                                                         These machine learning roadmaps are specifically trained on proprietary annotated
                                                                         datasets with human reinforcement feedback loops, and with outputs that are




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                         deterministic. These datasets in turn have been built and maintained by proprietary data
                                                                         collection from control points at the network layer or endpoint layer that cannot be
                                                                         replicated by synthetic data to the best of our knowledge. In some cases, proprietary
                                                                         data collection happens via human assets using very deep tradecraft. In our meeting
                                                                         with Adam Meyers, SVP of Counter Intelligence at CrowdStrike on April 7, Mr. Meyers
                                                                         noted CrowdStrike’s internal runway of AI innovation and the frontier lab runway of AI
                                                                         innovation as independent. This dynamic further reinforces our view that a security
                                                                         vendor leveraging AI on its specialized data and then applying it to unique customer
                                                                         data should be able to deliver better and cheaper outcomes to customers, irrespective
                                                                         of where the performance of general purpose LLMs go over time.

                                                                         In contrast, frontier lab roadmaps are driven by generative AI. There is still
                                                                         opportunity to drive better security outcomes using generative AI, but we view the




                                                                                                                                                                        455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                         use cases as different. One of the most successful GenAI use cases to date has been in
                                                                         coding — as more code is generated, the focus shifts to how to make that code more
                                                                         secure. Importantly, improving security at the very first step of writing / building an
                                                                         application may meaningful reduce the potential for vulnerabilities to be exploited once
                                                                         the app is in production. We expect over time the LLM providers will focus on two
                                                                         runways: i) providing a base level of security within their product that limits any friction
                                                                         to adoption (similar to Microsoft with E5 security capabilities and AWS with inbuilt cloud
                                                                         security controls), ii) security use cases that could be solved with very strong anomaly
                                                                         detection engines and in spite of limited security specific domain experience. While
                                                                         LLMs may likely not build comprehensive endpoint, network, or SOC products; the same
                                                                         anomaly detection capabilities used for code security could theoretically extend into
                                                                         adjacent domains such as user behavior analytics, network traffic analysis, or SOC
                                                                         investigations. In the short term, we expect to see pressure in the code security and pen
                                                                         testing categories. In the medium term, we expect Security vendors partnering with
                                                                         LLMs will also impact categories within the Security Operations Center (such as SOAR
                                                                         and NDR).

                                                                                      2. Security leaders turn disruption risk into disruption opportunity via M&A
                                                                                      One of the main challenges in security is that technology is going up against active
                                                                                      adversaries that are looking to undermine the parameters on which the products
                                                                                      are built. This leads to the R&D process being more revolutionary than evolutionary —
                                                                                      companies can’t simply make the next version of their product faster and better and
                                                                                      expect it to be successful, as the threat vectors evolve too quickly. In our view, one of the
                                                                                      more successful ways of compounding a competitive moat is therefore via M&A, and
                                                                                      specifically acquiring next-gen security companies that are early on in their growth. This
                                                                                      allows the acquirer to more easily integrate the technology stack and team, and benefit
                                                                                      from the revenue synergies of selling the new product across its existing installed base
                                                                                      via its scaled go-to-market motion.

                                                                                      This M&A playbook is being executed real time, and at a faster pace than in the
                                                                                      cloud security cycle (Exhibit 8). IaaS had already been scaling for several years before
                                                                                      the cloud-security M&A wave. If we use 2015 as the start of the more meaningful IaaS
                                                                                      ramp, the acquisition wave came four to five years later. Wiz, which became the largest
                                                                                      independent cloud-security winner, was not founded until 2020. Palo Alto has made the
                                                                                      cloud comparison directly, but the starting point is different. In 2018/2019, the




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                                      company was using M&A to build out cloud security and shift the portfolio away from
                                                                                      on-prem. In AI, Palo Alto already has many of the relevant control points across network,
                                                                                      endpoint, cloud, browser, and identity. The issue is extending those controls into AI

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                  indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
                                                  For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
