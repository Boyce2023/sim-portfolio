                                                  SK Holdings is the operating holding company of the SK group, the 3rd largest non-SOE conglomerate in Korea in
                                                  terms of assets (group assets of c.US$140bn as of April 2016). The SK group started with textile manufacturing in 1938,

                                                  currently has business across 28 different industries including refining, chemicals, telecommunications, IT service,
                                                  semiconductor, utility, construction, shipping, logistics, property management, used car distribution, and trading.

                                                  PM Summary: Expanding NAV through virtuous cash flow cycle
                                                                                   Initiate with Buy: We initiate coverage on SK Holdings with a Buy rating and 12-month
                                                                                   target price of W320,000, implying 19% upside. SK Holdings is the operating holding
                                                                                   company of the SK group, the 3rd largest non-SOE Korean conglomerate in terms of assets,
                                                                                   following the Samsung group and Hyundai Motor group. SK Holdings has a balanced
                                                                                   portfolio of assets operating in the IT services/telecom/semiconductor, energy, and
                                                                                   pharmaceutical industries. We believe there are sufficient catalysts to further improve
                                                                                   shareholder value in the next 12 months. In contrast to Samsung and Hyundai Motor, the
                                                                                   SK group has already transitioned to a pure holding company structure without any
                                                                                   circular ownership loop.

                                                                                   Accelerating CROCI driven by virtuous cash flow cycle: Dividends and brand royalties
                                                                                   from group subsidiaries SK E&S, SK Innovation, and SK Telecom accounted for the
                                                                                   majority of SK Holdings’ DACF in 2016 and cash flow has been reinvested in higher ROE-
                                                                                   and CROCI-generating pharmaceuticals, logistics, and semiconductor-related businesses.
                                                                                   The group’s acquisitions in the past have already contributed to substantial wealth creation

                                                  Valuation: NAV-based SOTP implies 19% upside
                                                                                   Whereas our published forecasts for SK Holdings are for the parent company alone in
                                                                                   order to provide a more transparent fundamental perspective on the parent company
                                                                                   operation, we use a sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuation to calculate our 12-month target
                                                                                   price, in order to incorporate the asset value of subsidiaries. Our SOTP valuation includes
                                                                                   the following:

                                                                                   1)   Listed assets: 41% of NAV comes from six listed subsidiaries. We used the market
                                                                                        price as of September 21, 2017 for each.
                                                                                   2)   Unlisted assets: 7% of NAV comes from unlisted subsidiaries with less than 100%
                                                                                        ownership. For subsidiaries with a prior transaction record (SK Encar.com’s stake sale
                                                                                        to Carsales Holdings in Australia, acquisition of SK SiItron from LG Corp. and ESR from
                                                                                        e-Shang Redwood Group), we used the transaction price for our SOTP valuation. For
                                                                                        subsidiaries without a recent transaction record, we used 1.0X book value (implying a
                                                                                        0.7X P/B multiple given the 30% discount to our NAV-based SOTP valuation) as we
                                                                                        believe the scope for those businesses to collectively generate over 10% ROE is

                                                                                        businesses such as IT services and used cars, and from brand royalties and dividend
                                                                                        income) as well as wholly owned subsidiaries operating in different industries. We
                                                                                        derive their fair value based on peer P/E and DCF (except SK Forrest, for which we use

                                                                                             SK Biopharm (7% of our NAV-based SOTP valuation) is a 100% owned subsidiary of SK

                                                                                             psychiatry, and brain tumor drugs with an addressable market size of US$92bn. The
                                                                                             company has R&D facilities in Korea and the US with 9 projects currently in the clinical
                                                                                             development pipeline. Our focus is on phase 3 trials of YKP3089 (epilepsy drug) and SKL-
                                                                                             N05 (sleep disorder drug).

                                                                                             For YKP3089, an epilepsy drug for focal and generalized onset seizures, the company
                                                                                             targets W2.7bn in peak sales in 2030. The US accounts for 67% of the global market for
                                                                                             epilepsy treatments, and patients usually take 2-3 different drugs at the same time. Patients
                                                                                             tend to prefer new drugs, price elasticity is low, and drug prices are subject to only mild
                                                                                             regulation in the US and Europe. In addition to YKP3089, SK Biopharm plans to start phase
                                                                                             2 trials of Carisbamate (for unknown onset seizure), targeting an NDA in 2021.

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our coverage universe) and
                                                  its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock.
                                                  The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each
                                                  metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile
                                                  indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

                                                  M&A Rank
                                                  Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may
                                                  vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of
                                                  scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition
                                                  target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our
                                                  standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore
                                                  does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
