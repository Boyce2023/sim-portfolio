                                                  Executive Summary

                                                                                            Traditional robotics have been successful at automating certain tasks, but limits
                                                                                            with respect to software capability and hardware flexibility have historically
                                                                                            constrained adoption to certain areas that are more repetitive in nature. We expect
                                                                                            this to change going forward due to physical AI. Physical AI, per Nvidia, is what
                                                                                            enables machines such as robots and AVs to perceive, understand, and perform complex
                                                                                            actions in the physical world.

                                                                                            Physical AI technology for robots is improving, and humanoid robots from several
                                                                                            providers are increasingly set to move into larger scale prototypes and toward
                                                                                            commercial production. This is being enabled in part as companies gather real world
                                                                                            data to train AI software, including for both general reasoning and specific tasks (such as
                                                                                            how to pick up an envelope or break an egg), which can’t be done simply with digital
                                                                                            data from the internet. The industry is also befitting from new open-sourced tools, rising
                                                                                            funding, and government support. We also believe increasing deployments can help
                                                                                            drive a positive flywheel effect, with the robots in operation helping gather data to

                                                                                            more general purpose in utility over time, including humanoids.

                                                                                            890K in 2030 and 6.5 mn in 2035 on a units basis, up from 256K and 1.4 mn prior. On a

                                                  The frontier of robotics tech – humanoids and foundational models


                                                                        AI technology continues to improve with focus areas including data collection, world
                                                                        models and safety
                                                                        AI model performance continues to improve. One measure of progress with AI models
                                                                        is the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI), which is a generalized benchmark intended to
                                                                        capture the overall capability of AI models by aggregating performance across several
                                                                        established tests covering reasoning, coding, math, and language. Unlike individual
                                                                        benchmarks which can saturate as models approach near‑perfect scores, ECI adjusts for
                                                                        task difficulty and combines results to show relative differentiation across models and
                                                                        over time. Higher scores indicate broader capability, while models optimized for specific
                                                                        tests may perform well on individual benchmarks but rank lower on ECI due to limited
                                                                        generalization.

                                                                        Models launched in 2023 were scoring around the low to mid 120s range, while models
                                                                        released in 2026 (GPT-5.4 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.6) are scoring in the mid
                                                                        to high 150s range.

                                                                          C) Korea: Two Primary Roles in the Global Robotics Ecosystem

                                                                          Key Enablers for Humanoid Developers (Ex-China): South Korea is uniquely positioned
                                                                          as a critical supply-chain partner for non-Chinese humanoid developers, leveraging its
                                                                          robust automotive-parts ecosystem to lead in humanoid actuator production. US-based
                                                                          developers are increasingly sourcing these critical components from Korean suppliers.
                                                                          For example, Boston Dynamics selected Hyundai Mobis to supply actuators for its
                                                                          next-generation Atlas humanoid, while HL Mando—already a supplier of quadruped
                                                                          robot actuators in the US—is preparing for a pivot into humanoid actuator mass
                                                                          production in the US. The country also features a mature domestic robotics ecosystem
                                                                          of established players and agile startups. Robotis (founded in 1999) is expanding its
                                                                          global footprint by preparing for mass actuator production at its new Uzbekistan facility.
                                                                          Additionally, South Korea has a high concentration of developers specializing in
                                                                          dexterous robotic hands, a capability that evolved from its strong industrial gripper
                                                                          manufacturing base.

                                                                          humanoid robotics, driven by its dense manufacturing base, early-adopter culture, and
                                                                          aggressive government backing. With the world’s highest industrial robot density across
                                                                          the semiconductor, automotive, shipbuilding, and steel sectors, South Korea provides a
                                                                          vast repository of physical manufacturing and operational data. This real-world data is
                                                                          vital for training AI models and bridging the “sim-to-real” gap. This high exposure has
                                                                          also fostered strong public acceptance and a tech-forward culture. Rapid population
                                                                          aging and a heavy reliance on manufacturing create an urgent domestic need for

                                                  Robotics and Physical AI Survey highlights

                                                                                     We recently launched our first Executive Perspectives powered by Goldman Sachs
                                                                                     survey, a new global initiative that looks to capture insights directly from companies
                                                                                     across our global coverage. For our first edition we received responses from >80
                                                                                     companies globally, across multiple industries, including industrials, autos, healthcare,
                                                                                     utilities, consumer, and more, on robotics and physical AI, including humanoids. The
                                                                                     survey gathered insights on adoption trends, investment priorities, and potential
                                                                                     implications for productivity and financials over the next several years.

                                                                                     Some key takeaways include:

                                                                                     n    ~30-35% of respondents are seriously evaluating/piloting use cases of general

                                                                                          deployments
                                                                                     n    ~70% of respondents currently use or expect to use general purpose and or
                                                                                          humanoids for manufacturing/production.

                                                  Warehouse automation - large potential to leverage a range of robotics and AI technology

                                                                                                        We believe warehouse and logistics operations are a microcosm for automation, with
                                                                                                        deployments of various technologies including traditional robotic arms, machine
                                                                                                        vision/scanning tech, AMRs, and humanoids. As we discuss in this section, the market is
                                                                                                        likely still in the early innings of adoption, and we expect growing demand, in part from
                                                                                                        labor/supply chain trends.


                                                                                                        Warehouse automation remains underpenetrated, implies substantial upside potential
                                                                                                        for humanoids and other automation solution suppliers
                                                                                                        Automation penetration in warehousing and logistics remains low relative to
                                                                                                        manufacturing verticals where robotics adoption is mature. The clearest reference point
                                                                                                        is automotive, where decades of investment have lifted robot density to nearly 1,800
                                                                                                        units per 10,000 employees, versus 250 across non-automotive manufacturing
                                                                                                        industries (Exhibit 22). This comparison is directional rather than a direct measure of
                                                                                                        warehouse automation, as IFR or any other automation statistics do not fully capture

                                                                                                            automated warehouse/logistics systems. Nevertheless, the concentration of robot
                                                                                                            adoption in standardized manufacturing processes highlights the substantial automation
                                                                                                            headroom remaining in labor-intensive warehouse activities.

                                                                                                            Financials from our covered companies support this view - Daifuku, the global leader in
                                                                                                            material-handling systems, has compounded revenue at 10% over the past 15 years,
                                                                                                            materially ahead of the broader Japan capital-goods universe (Exhibit 23), or industrial

                                                                                                            Logistics costs have faced and will face structural upward pressure
                                                                                                            Logistics costs are subject to increasingly structural pressure from labor shortages, wage
                                                                                                            inflation, logistics pricing, and higher inventory intensity. The trend is particularly visible
                                                                                                            in Japan. Industry data shows warehouse operating costs rising over recent years
                                                                                                            (Exhibit 24), while the economy-wide logistics-cost ratio, after declining for much of the
                                                                                                            prior two decades, has re-inflected toward historically elevated levels (Exhibit 25).

                                                                                                            The composition of the increase is important. Inventory-related costs are near historical
                                                                                                            peaks, with the burden concentrated in raw materials and work-in-progress rather than
                                                                                                            finished goods (Exhibit 27). This does not translate one-for-one into greater picking
                                                                                                            volume, but it does increase storage, handling, location management, sorting, and
                                                                                                            production-staging requirements, which are workflows directly addressable through
                                                                                                            automated storage and retrieval, conveyance, and sortation systems.

                                                                                                            The US trend is less pronounced on the available inventory-management measures
                                                                                                            (Exhibit 31). Japan therefore appears to face the more immediate pressure, while also
                                                                                                            providing a potential leading indicator of the operational challenges that tighter labor
                                                                                                            markets and higher service requirements could create elsewhere.

                                                                                                                                Rising work content and operating complexity tailwind for automation




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                                                                                The second structural driver is the expanding operating scale managed by each
                                                                                                                                warehouse employee. Data shows floor area and revenue per worker increasing over
                                                                                                                                time (Exhibit 32). These measures do not necessarily imply greater physical workload in
                                                                                                                                isolation, but they indicate that warehouse operators must manage more activity and
                                                                                                                                capacity with a more expensive labor base.

                                                                                                                                Demand-side complexity compounds the pressure. MonotaRO’s expanding product
                                                                                                                                line-up and inventory SKU count illustrate the growth in assortment breadth (Exhibit 33),
                                                                                                                                while TRUSCO reports rising inventory items alongside higher labor cost per shipment
                                                                                                                                line (Exhibit 34). Shorter delivery lead times have also shifted from a competitive
                                                                                                                                differentiator toward a baseline customer requirement across manufacturing and
                                                                                                                                distribution markets.

                                                                                                                                More SKUs, fragmented order profiles, faster turnaround requirements, and rising labor




                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                                                                                costs make continued expansion through manual labor increasingly difficult. We
                                                                                                                                therefore see the need for warehouse productivity investment as structural rather than
                                                                                                                                cyclical. The remaining question is not whether automation penetration rises, but which
                                                                                                                                solutions—fixed automation, AMRs, robotic manipulators or humanoids—are best
                                                                                                                                suited to each remaining workflow.

                                                                   What are virtual PLCs and how do they compare to standard PLCs?
                                                                   A Programmable Logic Controller (PLC) is a microprocessor-based industrial
                                                                   computer engineered to control and monitor manufacturing processes. Since their
                                                                   introduction in the late 1960s, PLCs have served as the backbone of factory automation,
                                                                   executing real-time control logic to ensure the seamless coordination of assembly lines
                                                                   and machinery. At its core, a PLC acts as the central orchestrator, continuously
                                                                   monitoring inputs from field sensors, processing this data according to a user-defined
                                                                   program, and executing precise commands to drive outputs such as motors, valves and
                                                                   actuators. For example, on a robotic assembly line, while an individual robot utilizes its
                                                                   own dedicated controller to manage precise joint movements, a PLC will sit above the
                                                                   controller, coordinating the robot’s actions with surrounding equipment such as




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                   conveyors and safety barriers. Historically, market-leading automation providers such as
                                                                   Siemens, Rockwell Automation, Mitsubishi Electric, and Schneider Electric, have sold
                                                                   PLCs under a hardware-centric business model, requiring a dedicated physical controller
                                                                   for each machine or robot. This architecture inherently creates vendor lock-in, forcing
                                                                   manufacturers to purchase proprietary hardware and rely on the vendor’s closed
                                                                   software ecosystems for programming and maintenance.

                                                                   Virtual PLCs (vPLCs) perform the same real-time control functions as traditional
                                                                   PLCs but are entirely software-defined. When a new assembly line needs to be
                                                                   controlled, traditional PLCs oblige operators to buy new hardware which needs to be
                                                                   mounted inside electrical cabinets and wired to machinery. By contrast, vPLCs allow
                                                                   operators to deploy a new “PLC” when needed without installing new physical
                                                                   controllers. This confers various advantages: 1) vPLCs can run on standard, off-the-shelf
                                                                   computer servers, or on the cloud, meaning operators are not locked into a proprietary




                                                                                                                                                                   455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                   hardware ecosystem and can mitigate supply chain risks, 2) manufacturers can expand
                                                                   production lines and scale capacity at lower capex, 3) operators can update control logic
                                                                   remotely from a centralised console, reducing the need for on-site engineers.

                                                                   The rapid development of humanoid robots has brought the advantages of vPLCs to
                                                                   the forefront of industrial automation. Unlike stationary industrial robots that rely on
                                                                   dedicated physical PLCs, mobile humanoids coordinate with their environments via
                                                                   software-defined vPLCs. This software-driven approach is essential because humanoid
                                                                   “brains” run on advanced AI models and computer vision algorithms powered by
                                                                   high-performance Systems-on-Chips (SoCs). Bridging these modern, software-defined
                                                                   platforms with rigid, hardware-bound physical PLCs is highly complex. vPLCs solve this
                                                                   integration bottleneck as they establish a centralized system which can directly control
                                                                   an entire fleet of mobile humanoids over high-speed virtual networks. This streamlines
                                                                   data exchange, simplifies the overall system architecture and enhances simultaneous
                                                                   coordination of distinct humanoids.

                                                                   grow at c. 4% CAGR over the coming years (GSe and third party sources Mordor
                                                                   Intelligence, Precedence Research, Global Market Insights), in line with historical growth

                                                                   Why do physical PLCs still dominate? What are the risks associated with vPLCs?
                                                                   Despite the rapid rise of software-defined automation, traditional physical PLCs remain
                                                                   superior to vPLCs in certain critical areas, explaining why they have not yet been fully
                                                                   replaced by virtual alternatives. These include latency, safety and complexity of
                                                                   deployment.

                                                                   n   Latency and determinism. Physical PLCs run on dedicated hardware with a
                                                                       real-time operating system which guarantees precise, deterministic,
                                                                       millisecond-level execution loops (typically 1-10 ms). By contrast, vPLCs operate on
                                                                       shared compute infrastructure where they must share CPU cycles with other
                                                                       applications. While vPLCs offer performance close to traditional PLCs, the
                                                                       introduction of virtualisation can result in additional and sometimes unpredictable
                                                                       latency. This poses a significant challenge for real-time applications that necessitate
                                                                       ultra-high-speed, synchronized response times such as high-speed motion control




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                       systems or high-speed packaging.
                                                                   n   Functional safety and reliability. Hosting multiple vPLCs on a single edge server
                                                                       introduces a potential single point of failure. If the host server experiences a
                                                                       hardware crash or power loss, it could result in simultaneous failures across several
                                                                       independent control systems, potentially halting operations across an entire factory
                                                                       floor. This is a significant risk for industries that rely on maximum availability and
                                                                       uptime, such as continuous process industries. While vPLCs have started to receive
                                                                       safety certifications (e.g. Siemens’ TUV-certified SIMATIC S7-1500V with failsafe
                                                                       function), this is only a recent development. These safety-certified vPLCs have not
                                                                       yet been deployed long enough in real-world environments to prove they can match
                                                                       the decades-long reliability record of traditional physical PLCs.
                                                                   n   Legacy plants and complexity of deployment. The vPLC market is still highly




                                                                                                                                                                   455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                       nascent, with many legacy plants operating stable, fixed automation architectures
                                                                       that have reliably run on physical PLCs for decades. This leaves little operational
                                                                       incentive to undergo complex and costly migrations to virtualized environments.
                                                                       Deploying and managing vPLCs involves a level of complexity far beyond traditional
                                                                       PLC systems. It requires deep expertise in virtualisation technologies, IT
                                                                       infrastructure and network security; domains that are often unfamiliar to traditional
                                                                       automation engineers. Overcoming this skills gap may require organisations to invest
                                                                       heavily in training existing workforce or hiring new talent in order to effectively
                                                                       manage vPLC infrastructure.


                                                                   How will the monetization model change?
                                                                   The transition to software-defined automation (SDA) and vPLCs fundamentally
                                                                   alters how automation OEMs like Siemens, Rockwell Automation, Mitsubishi
                                                                   Electric, and Schneider Electric monetize their offerings. In short, it shifts the
                                                                   industry from a capital expenditure based model to an operational expenditure and
                                                                   Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) model. Historically, manufacturers generated
                                                                   high-margin upfront revenues by selling proprietary physical hardware such as CPUs,


                                                  30 August 2026                                                                                             46
                                                  Goldman Sachs                                                                              Global Physical AI

                                                                   chassis, and I/O modules, bundled with proprietary engineering software licenses. With
                                                                   vPLCs, the physical hardware is decoupled and vendors instead monetize the technology
                                                                   through software subscription licenses, consumption-based fees, and managed services.
                                                                   While this subscription-based, pay-per-use model may dilute upfront hardware margins,
                                                                   it establishes highly predictable, recurring revenue streams. It also allows vendors to
                                                                   capture additional value by upselling centralized edge management platforms, remote
                                                                   diagnostics, and advanced AI-driven analytics. Siemens has decoupled its hardware and
                                                                   software offerings through its Siemens Xcelerator portfolio, offering its primary vPLC
                                                                   product (SIMATIC S7-1500V) under an annual subscription model. Schneider has also
                                                                   introduced its EcoStruxure Automation Expert platform which enables software-defined
                                                                   automation to run on any compatible hardware.

                                                                   Even players that transition to SaaS might face risks though, as with generative AI
                                                                   democratizing industrial programming it erodes the barriers to entry into the vPLC
                                                                   market and increases the risks from ‘seat compression’ of traditional SaaS industrials
                                                                   models, given the reduced need for headcount with licences. Historically, a factory floor
                                                                   required dozens of physical PLCs. With virtualization, multiple vPLCs can be
                                                                   consolidated onto a single edge server. As AI agents optimize factory-floor workloads,

                                                                   the demand will shift from paying “per vPLC instance” to paying for operational

                                                                   dynamic, value-based pricing risk losing market share to agile, cloud-native competitors.

                                                                   experience.

                                                                       levels while simultaneously lowering costs through reduced markdowns, lower

                                                  Software: World Models may drive the next leg of token demand

                                                                        Success with LLMs has catalyzed investment in World Models. We have had several
                                                                        industry conversations on the software stack for robotics in the last six months,
                                                                        including with founders at world model companies and academics at Stanford. We have
                                                                        two observations on why investment and progress is inflecting now:

                                                                        1. Meaningful progress has been made on LLM research, but the industry is running
                                                                           into limitation with the types of problems that LLMs can solve, as LLMs are limited to
                                                                           text-based datasets, and the field of LLM research is becoming more saturated.
                                                                        2. Researchers can apply learnings from LLMs to world model progressions. For


                                                  30 August 2026                                                                                                  55
                                                  Goldman Sachs                                                                              Global Physical AI

                                                                      example, newer world model companies like Physical Intelligence (PI) and Generalist
                                                                      AI focus on training a single model across highly diverse tasks and then applying that
                                                                      model to narrow use cases, in an approach that parallels frontier LLM models and in
                                                                      contrast to task-specific robots.

                                                                   The long-term moat is proprietary, deployment driven data. Locomotion and
                                                                   navigation algorithms are largely solved through simulation‑driven reinforcement
                                                                   learning. However, real‑world manipulation parameters (e.g., those tied to deformable
                                                                   objects, food preparation, tool use) are fundamentally un-simulatable and therefore
                                                                   data‑constrained. As a result, world model companies orient their research and capital
                                                                   toward tasks where real‑world interaction is unavoidable and where labor displacement
                                                                   potential is highest. Two examples:

                                                                   1. Physical Intelligence has detailed a closed‑loop system in which human‑operated
                                                                      robots generate video and action data for specific tasks, models are retrained and
                                                                      validated on live robots, and subsequent deployments generate incremental
                                                                      proprietary data. Critically, this proprietary dataset improves with each deployment,
                                                                      reinforcing management’s view that data accumulation is the primary competitive




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                      advantage.
                                                                   2. Generalist AI’s key differentiation is a) using visual images rather than sensors. The
                                                                      model observes an off-the-shelf commercial robot via cameras, converts the signal
                                                                      into tokens, and uses the AI world model to predict the next best movement for the
                                                                      robot arms in space; b) a focus on dexterity, scaling, tightly integrated feedback
                                                                      loops between research and deployment, and a talent-dense team.

                                                                   In our view, new world model companies illustrate that the technical breakthroughs
                                                                   in AI in recent years may have meaningful implications well beyond the enterprise
                                                                   software TAM that we typically focus on. This may create a new class of companies
                                                                   that span the best of two business models: the Software business model (where
                                                                   code is the cornerstone of driving model performance for real world applications)
                                                                   and those business models with a physical moat (which may be more defensible in




                                                                                                                                                                  455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                   an era where the cost of code is low).

                                                                   The token demand curve for Robotics will likely create another lever to longer-term
                                                                   data center demand dynamics. Recall we introduced our estimate that demand for
                                                                   tokens would increase 24x in the next 5 years in our agentic economy report. This
                                                                   estimate contemplates text to more complex imaging to more complex agentic step ups.

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile
                                                  indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
                                                  For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
