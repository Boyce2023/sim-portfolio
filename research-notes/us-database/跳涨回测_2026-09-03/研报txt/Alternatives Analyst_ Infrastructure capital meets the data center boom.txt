                                                  Infrastructure capital meets the data center boom

                                                                         Capex forecasts continue to rise

                                                                         The forecasts for artificial intelligence (AI) related capital expenditures continue to grow.
                                                                         Our colleagues in GS equity research now expect a combined $5.3 trillion of capex
                                                                         spending for the four largest hyperscalers (Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet)
                                                                         from FY2025 to FY2030. Prior to the start of 1Q2026 earnings, this figure stood at $4.5
                                                                         trillion. And a May 2026 report from the Goldman Sachs Global Institute highlighted a
                                                                         baseline aggregate capex estimate of $7.6 trillion between 2026 and 2031, across
                                                                         compute, data centers, and power.

                                                                         A range of financing markets—across public and private—will likely be required

                                                                         As we have highlighted previously, we expect a variety of financing markets (public,
                                                                         securitized, and private), structures (including new ‘project finance style’ JVs), and
                                                                         currencies will be required to satisfy the scale and scope of this funding need. Both the
                                                                         depth and breadth of the private financing ecosystem have already featured heavily in

                                                                         Blurring of the lines across ‘real assets’

                                                                         Notably, the lines between the ‘real asset’ categories of infrastructure and real estate
                                                                         appear to be blurring as multiple aspects of data center financing (i.e., land, operational
                                                                         network, power, building, and equipment) touch different asset classifications. Some
                                                                         data providers are adapting their taxonomy to adjust. Nevertheless, leaving the

                                                                  categories aside, it is the combined, long-term financing capacity across these
                                                                  categories that matters most for investors in the liquid corporate credit markets, in our
                                                                  view.

                                                                  The ability and willingness of the private markets to play a meaningful role in AI-related
                                                                  financing is important because we expect liquid credit market saturation and issuer

                                                                  pricing as the multi-year AI investment cycle continues.

                                                                  The case for accelerating AUM growth in infrastructure

                                                                  The asset class of infrastructure sits at the epicenter of multiple structural tailwinds
                                                                  which we expect will support its growth and provide additional capacity for financing.
                                                                  Beyond the digital- and energy-related investments required for the build-out of AI
                                                                  capabilities, we also see scope for new infrastructure investment related to energy
                                                                  security (following recent geopolitical conflicts, for example).




                                                                                                                                                                            455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                  And as inflation remains top of mind for a wide range of market participants,
                                                                  infrastructure’s structured income generation stream and inflation protection
                                                                  characteristics (including direct cost pass-throughs, in some instances) are likely to
                                                                  further support growth. Indeed, a November 2025 Preqin survey of global institutional
                                                                  investors found the most common reasons for allocating to infrastructure were the
                                                                  asset’s ability to provide: (1) diversification, (2) a reliable income stream, and (3) an
                                                                  inflation hedge (Exhibit 4). Our colleagues in global portfolio strategy research have also
                                                                  highlighted the case for allocating to real assets over the medium term, as a way to
                                                                  balance exposure to innovation with protection from inflation.

                                                                  New annual records for fundraising and deal sizes

                                                                  As Exhibit 6 shows, infrastructure fundraising reached a new annual record in 2025, with
                                                                  $221 billion raised. At the same time, the average fund size jumped to $1.8 billion. This is
                                                                  a direct result, in our view, of the concentrated nature of the fundraising landscape, as
                                                                  allocators favor managers with scale and strong track records. This pattern is similar to
                                                                  what we have observed in other private asset classes, such as private credit, where
                                                                  scaled and experienced managers have been raising larger funds and capturing the
                                                                  majority of the capital.

                                                                  Data from Preqin shows that infrastructure funds are not only growing in size, but they




                                                                                                                                                                                                      455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                  are also closing more quickly. For example, over the course of 2018 to 2021, the largest
                                                                  infrastructure funds spent 13 to 18 months (on average) fundraising. From 2022 to
                                                                  3Q2025, however, the largest funds spent 7 to 12 months (on average) fundraising. As of
                                                                  May 2026, there were 695 infrastructure funds (globally) in market fundraising, targeting
                                                                  an aggregate $555 billion of capital.
