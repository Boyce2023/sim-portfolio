                                                  Why the Reliability investment theme is still compelling

                                                                          The combination of supply disruptions and sharply higher prices of global oil and
                                                                          Europe/Asia natural gas caused by the US-Iran war is likely to hasten infrastructure
                                                                          investment in redundancy and bring more investor focus on the theme of Reliability,
                                                                          even after the disruptions subside. As we highlighted in our December 2025 report,
                                                                          “The Reliability & Livelihood Imperatives: Investment themes for 2026,” heightened
                                                                          consumer and policymaker focus on maintaining the Reliability of Power, Water, Supply
                                                                          Chains, Labor and Networks, is driving a surge in investment. This trend is fueled by AI
                                                                          expansion, aging infrastructure, reshoring, higher/more extreme temperatures and
                                                                          demographic shifts.

                                                                          Three factors should continue to drive the infrastructure investment up-cycle in
                                                                          2026 as corporates, governments and households add redundancy to mitigate risks
                                                                          of outages to power, water, labor, networks and supply chains:

                                                                          1. Acceleration in US/Europe power demand to levels not seen since the 1990s (3.2%
                                                                             US CAGR through 2030), providing the first Energy demand-driven up-cycle since

                                                                             2003-07.

                                                                             resulting in reshoring. We believe recent Middle East disruptions will only further
                                                                             increase interest among corporates, governments and households in adding
                                                                             redundancy to mitigate disruption risk.
                                                                          3. Weather-related disruptions amid aging infrastructure.

                                                                          Impact of Middle East disruptions. The push towards redundancy-building has been
                                                                          further fueled by Middle East energy disruptions, with three key implications:

                                                                          1. A greater move towards all-of-the-above energy sourcing decisions, with varied
                                                                             weightings by country based on domestic resources. Overall, we see a greater

                                                                             battery storage, and ensuring adequate capacity of on-the-margin power from
                                                                             sources such as natural gas. However, we expect gradations to vary by country based
                                                                             on domestic resources and confidence in supply chain dependencies. In the US,
                                                                             where natural gas and power prices have not been meaningfully impacted by global

                                                                             lower earth orbit monitoring demand and utility/water infrastructure.
                                                                          3. Affordability challenges, particularly for lower-income households, will likely fuel

                                                                  How long can the Reliability supercycle last? In our view, the resulting Reliability
                                                                  supercycle (>$80 bn of annual capex growth based on our estimates from public
                                                                  companies with Green Capex tailwinds despite sunsetting US IRA incentives) is poised to
                                                                  continue until one or more of three shifts occur:

                                                                  1. AI impact is sufficiently defined to no longer be perceived as a competitive threat
                                                                     that requires aggressive investment. Meetings from our trip suggested continued
                                                                     pent-up demand for AI compute and tokens — that productivity gains for
                                                                     chips/servers/models are unlikely to drive lower tech R&D budgets but rather
                                                                     increased demand for compute/tokens.

                                                                  2. Erosion of corporate (in particular hyperscaler) returns, free cash flow and balance
                                                                     sheet strength that would diminish the >$1 tn of spare capacity available for
                                                                     additional investment across all sectors based on our estimates. A deceleration or
                                                                     decline in economic growth would represent a risk. While our analysts’ estimates
                                                                     imply hyperscalers’ cash return on cash invested degradation in the coming years,
                                                                     projected CROCI is still above the low end of the prior 10-year range, and balance
                                                                     sheets are strong (0.2x 2026E net debt/2027E EBITDA).
                                                                  3. Redundancy to mitigate risk of outages to power, water, labor, networks and supply
                                                                     chains is perceived as sufficient. As mentioned, companies on our trip described
                                                                     increased efforts towards government security-driven stockpiling of critical
                                                                     resources, government incentives to encourage reshoring and corporate investment
                                                                     towards infrastructure resiliency and physical risk mitigation.

                                                                        We met with Byron Jeffers, VP of Finance, Treasury, and IR, as well as Alain Mokbel,

                                                                        What we learned at the meeting
                                                                        Demand remains strong but looking to realize higher pricing from Section 232.
                                                                        Management discussed that the current demand environment remains strong and that it
                                                                        could be booking more volumes. However, the company is looking to realize higher
                                                                        pricing as it continues to take a disciplined approach to bookings ahead of a potential
                                                                        outcome from the ongoing Section 232 investigation for polysilicon. While there
                                                                        continues to be market speculation in terms of the potential outcome of the
                                                                        investigation, FSLR continues to advocate for a fixed price construct as well as no import
                                                                        quotas. FSLR views this as an opportunity to capture incremental pricing power, but
                                                                        remains mindful on fostering long-term customer relationships and is unlikely to be too




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                        aggressive on its pricing strategy. In aggregate, the company sees a plethora of policy
                                                                        initiatives such as Section 301 tariffs, AD/CVD tariffs, 45X manufacturing credits, among
                                                                        others, as supportive of the government’s focus to support the domestication of
                                                                        manufacturing capacity.

                                                                        Technology innovation driving upside. FSLR is actively advancing technology
                                                                        innovations, including CuRe (Copper Replacement) technology as well as perovskite
                                                                        capacity. Specifically, FSLR expects CuRe to drive an ~8% lifetime uplift in energy yield
                                                                        over TOPCon technology and is expected to be replicated across its Series 6 and 7 fleet
                                                                        through 1H28, with the first facility (India) occurring in 2027. In aggregate, the company
                                                                        estimates a ~$0.6 bn revenue uplift from tech adjusters between 2027 and 2028, albeit
                                                                        at a lower gross margin than its core business but views the opportunity as neutral to
                                                                        accretive to earnings. Management noted that it has previously broken out this upside
                                                                        when quoting booking ASPs but is now fully including the pricing for sales to be




                                                                                                                                                                        455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                        delivered in 2029 and beyond. Regarding perovskites, the company is advancing a 1 GW
                                                                        pilot facility in 2027, leveraging equipment from its southeast Asia operations —
                                                                        management noted that it has flexibility both for a lower initial output as well as to ramp
                                                                        over time based on deployment success.

                                                                        Managing supply chain and inflation. Despite persisting inflation and rising commodity
                                                                        costs, FSLR estimates that 2/3 of its BOM is composed of variable costs such as glass and
                                                                        frames. While tellurium prices remain elevated, FSLR is able to manage these risks by
                                                                        maintaining a relatively simple BOM without a multifaceted product portfolio.
                                                                        Additionally, the company is primarily selling its India volumes within the country, similar
                                                                        with its US domestic volumes, and is thus less exposed to freight volatility.


                                                                        Our view
                                                                        We view FSLR as the leading domestic manufacturer of solar modules, leveraging
                                                                        cadmium telluride (CdTe) technology. The company maintains the bulk of its
                                                                        manufacturing capacity within the US, with some volume in India as well as idled
                                                                        capacity in Southeast Asia. FSLR currently benefits from protectionist policy measures
                                                                        such as tariffs, manufacturing tax credits, as well as FEOC restrictions. Over the

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile
                                                  indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
                                                  For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
