                                                                  Hyperscaler commentary underscores the role of debt financing
                                                                  While the exact magnitude and mix of future debt issuance from the hyperscalers is
                                                                  uncertain, our review of management commentary leaves us expecting a growing role
                                                                  for debt financing in the AI buildout in the years ahead. Five key themes stand out to us,
                                                                  from our review of recent earnings call commentary and SEC filings.

                                                                  1. Expect meaningful increase in the pace of CapEx spending. “Significant
                                                                     investments” are expected to meet this “consequential” and “massive opportunity.”
                                                                     Recent upward revisions to CapEx guidance reflect “an acceleration in the delivery of

                                                                     capacity to meet growing demand.” CapEx in 2027 is expected to “increase
                                                                     significantly” per one hyperscaler. Right now, the environment is still “supply
                                                                     constrained.” One firm sees “exceptional demand on a moving-forward basis” and
                                                                     will “plan and invest accordingly” with “confidence.” With the backdrop “healthier
                                                                     than where we were about a year ago,” firms are “investing aggressively” to add
                                                                     capacity and meet infrastructure needs. One firm noted that it has “continued to
                                                                     underestimate” its compute needs.
                                                                  2. Timing mismatch of spending vs. cash flow generation. Cash flow from operations
                                                                     is the “first source of funding” for CapEx, but debt and equity will also play a role.
                                                                     Free cash flow will be “challenged” as CapEx outpaces revenue growth and given the
                                                                     delay in monetization (anywhere from three months to two years).

                                                                     Reflecting the significance of these costs, there were some references to planned

                                                                     hyperscalers also pay a modest cash dividend, which we expect will be maintained

                                                                  4. Return on investment should be well above a higher cost of debt capital. One

                                                                  The market and regional mix
                                                                  We estimate 65% to 75% of this debt will price in the USD IG bond market, subject to
                                                                  market conditions and investor receptivity in light of issuer concentration
                                                                  considerations. We see scope for the EUR market to absorb as much as 15% to 20% of
                                                                  the 2027 supply, especially since YTD2026 AI-related issuance in that market has lagged
                                                                  other regions (Exhibit 3).

                                                                  Over the course of FY2028, FY2029, and FY2030, we expect 33%, 30%, and 25% of




                                                                                                                                                                                                     455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                  hyperscaler CapEx will be debt financed, respectively. This translates into a potential
                                                                  $1.3 trillion of aggregate financing for hyperscaler CapEx. But as we first highlighted in
                                                                  April, we believe issuer concentration and market saturation considerations in the USD
                                                                  IG market will be more binding in the years ahead, as the hyperscalers will have likely
                                                                  grown to be among the largest issuers in the USD IG Corporate Index, by that point.

                                                                  For context, the three largest individual debt issuers (today) within the Bloomberg USD
                                                                  IG Corporate Index have between $165 billion and $175 billion (each) in index-eligible
                                                                  debt outstanding, and none of these issuers represent more than 2.3% of the index
                                                                  market value.

                                                                  This means that while the USD IG bond market will have likely absorbed the majority of
                                                                  the 2025-2027 hyperscaler issuance, this is unlikely to be the case over the longer term.
                                                                  Moreover, the smaller IG bond markets, such as CAD, CHF, GBP, JPY, and AUD, are likely
                                                                  to have exhausted their debt capacity by that point as well, given their very small sizes.
                                                                  As Exhibit 4 illustrates, the combined size of the Bloomberg GBP, JPY, CAD, AUD and CHF
                                                                  IG Corporate indices is $1.3 trillion. This compares to $3.0 trillion for the Bloomberg EUR
                                                                  IG Corporate Index, and $8.0 trillion for the Bloomberg USD IG Corporate Index.

                                                                  Framing the balance sheet and rating capacity for additional leverage




                                                                                                                                                                                                       455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                  Most of the hyperscalers have very low net leverage levels, owing to the significant
                                                                  amounts of cash on hand relative to gross debt balances and their EBITDA generation.
                                                                  And using the most recently reported metrics, even their gross leverage metrics are low
                                                                  by the standards of IG-rated non-financial firms, with the four lowest ranging between
                                                                  0.6x and 1.3x. As a result, these firms are very highly rated—near, or at, the top end of
                                                                  the spectrum for the IG category, in the AAA or AA cohort.

                                                                  Based on these metrics, our simple ‘back of the envelope’ math suggests extraordinary
                                                                  debt capacity for this group within the likely hypothetical confines of an IG debt rating.
                                                                  For example, we estimate between $1.3 trillion and $1.4 trillion of incremental debt
                                                                  capacity if these four hyperscalers pushed to 2.0x net leverage or 3.0x gross leverage—
                                                                  which we view as very conservative assumptions for a ‘middle-to-upper end’ range of
                                                                  IG-rated leverage. Furthermore, debt capacity should continue to grow as earnings
                                                                  increase. Using FY2027 EBITDA Bloomberg consensus estimates, those same target
                                                                  leverage metrics (2.0x net, and 3.0x gross) imply anywhere between $1.9 trillion and
                                                                  $2.3 trillion of incremental debt capacity.

                                                                  Again, while we do not expect all of this debt to be issued in the traditional IG corporate
                                                                  bond markets, there is scope, in our view, for the hyperscalers to lean more into broader
