                                                      Governor Abbott’s August 3 Directive: Data Center Interconnection Audit
                                                      What happened?

                                                      On August 3, 2026, Texas Governor Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT)
                                                      and ERCOT to conduct a comprehensive verification and audit of all data centers advancing through
                                                      ERCOT’s interconnection process. Notably, this audit must be completed before any data center project
                                                      moves forward, and any project failing to comply with PUCT/ERCOT requirements or state law will be
                                                      denied grid connection. The directive was prompted by an unprecedented ~474 GW of interconnection
                                                      requests (more than five times Texas’ record peak demand), of which ~90% are data centers, raising




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                      concerns about grid reliability and stability.

                                                      Building on his June 10 directive, the Governor also required each project to disclose five categories of
                                                      information:

                                                     n   Reliance on state/local financial assistance and tax incentives
                                                     n   The extent to which they provide their own power versus depending on the ERCOT grid, including
                                                         on-site generation efforts
                                                     n   Water sourcing, consumption, and cooling technology
                                                     n   Measures to reduce impacts on neighboring communities (noise, light, traffic, etc.)
                                                     n   Ownership and controlling interests in the project

                                                      In response, ERCOT immediately postponed its Batch Zero transmission planning study, effectively




                                                                                                                                                                           455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                      pausing the fast-track approval process for large loads.

                                                      Potential Implications to NRG and the Texas IPPs

                                                      We view the directive as broadly aligned with, rather than a threat to, NRG’s bring your own power (BYOP)
                                                      model. The five disclosure requirements align with NRG’s strategy around addressing hyperscaler load:
                                                      bringing new dedicated generation (addressing the own power criterion), responsible water stewardship
                                                      and cooling considerations, community protection measures, and transparency on ownership. Because
                                                      NRG’s project pairs the data center load with more generation than it consumes, reduces incremental
                                                      transmission needs, and places the investment burden on an investment-grade customer, it could screen
                                                      favorably under the audit framework. Additionally, the project’s late-2029 COD provides ample runway to
                                                      navigate the audit process, and management has noted the plant retains behind-the-meter (island mode)
                                                      flexibility, further insulating it from interconnection-related risk.

                                                      More broadly, the directive is likely to slow or disqualify grid-dependent, speculative data center loads,
                                                      which should tighten the effective interconnection queue, reward developers that bring their own supply,
                                                      and reinforce the scarcity value of IPPs with real development positions and dispatchable generation. The
                                                      near-term risk is heightened uncertainty and potential timeline delays across the sector, which we believe

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile

                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
