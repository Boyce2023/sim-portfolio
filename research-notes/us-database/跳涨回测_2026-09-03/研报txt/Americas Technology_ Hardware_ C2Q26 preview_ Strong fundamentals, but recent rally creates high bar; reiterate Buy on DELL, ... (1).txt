                                                  How much could agentic AI up lift the traditional compute market?

                                                                         Background: Last quarter, US server vendors & chip makers posted massive beats (DELL
                                                                         reported traditional server growth of +92% year-over-year v. consensus expectations
                                                                         for teens % growth) for traditional servers and raised outlook significantly for the full
                                                                         year, driven by both higher pricing and stronger unit demand from both (1) the refresh
                                                                         of existing enterprise infrastructure and (2) net-new agentic AI demand. Industry
                                                                         outlook for the traditional server market was also raised significantly, with 650 Group
                                                                         now expecting the market to nearly double by the end of the decade to $164 bn (up
                                                                         55% from previous outlook for 2030). Heading into C2Q earnings, investors are looking
                                                                         to understand how much further upside there could be to traditional servers revenue
                                                                         (both from ASP and unit demand perspective) across DELL, HPE, and SMCI, especially
                                                                         when contemplating the ongoing constraints on key components including DRAM and
                                                                         NAND.

                                                                         Why are CPUs better for agentic AI? Agentic AI requires a hybrid approach that blends
                                                                         the probabilistic reasoning of GPUs (statistical pattern recognition and generating




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                         tokens) with the deterministic execution of CPUs (rule-based logic, API tool calls, and
                                                                         strict workflow orchestration). Because agentic workflows are heavily tool-dominated
                                                                         and require constant, predictable orchestration, CPUs are better suited to handle these
                                                                         deterministic tasks to prevent latency bottlenecks. In June, we hosted former Intel
                                                                         executive Jordan Plawner for deep dive on CPU server trends. In addition to their
                                                                         strength at sequential processing, Mr. Plawner also noted that CPUs have direct access
                                                                         to DRAM, which makes them more efficient as feeding more data into AI systems. He
                                                                         sees agentic AI as driving a 5-6x uplift to the CPU server market as the market evolved
                                                                         from training towards inference/agentic workloads, with CPU:GPU ratios moving from
                                                                         1:4 towards 1:1 over time.

                                                                         Why will enterprises need to invest in on-premise infrastructure (v. cloud) for
                                                                         agentic AI? In July, we hosted former Microsoft director William Fong for a deep dive on
                                                                         AI infrastructure trends, where he highlighted his expectations for agentic AI to drive




                                                                                                                                                                      455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                         workloads on premise because of better cost, latency, and governance. To optimize
                                                                         across these constraints, Mr. Fong has observed organizations deploying a hybrid
                                                                         compute strategy where they are increasingly running individual AI agents locally
                                                                         on-premise to achieve millisecond-level latency and maintain strict data privacy, while
                                                                         utilizing the cloud for the complex multi-agent orchestration layers.

                                                                         What about refreshing the existing installed base of traditional servers? In addition
                                                                         to the agentic AI tailwind, the enterprise server market should also benefit from the
                                                                         refresh of its sizable aged installed base. In our Traditional and accelerated server
                                                                         industry primer published June 22, 2026, we highlighted the benefits of refreshing to
                                                                         new systems to increase the efficiency and power consumption. Latest generation CPU
                                                                         servers offer significant performance and efficiency improvements as compared to the
                                                                         vast majority of the installed base, driven by a ~4x higher core counts and ~2x denser
                                                                         memory/core. For cloud service providers, this densification is important because it
                                                                         addresses issues around power constraints, which is one of the limiting factors of overall
                                                                         compute growth. For enterprise, refreshing installed servers is also prerequisite for
                                                                         running agentic workflows, especially because running agents 24/7 will further
                                                                         accelerate the mean time to failure for servers, which is very costly for enterprises.

                                                  Is demand for AI servers broadening out from hyperscalers and neoclouds to sovereigns and
                                                  enterprise?

                                                                                                                     Background: AI infrastructure demand elevated across hyperscalers & neoclouds. Our
                                                                                                                     data center & utilities analysts updated the GS global data center capacity forecasts in
                                                                                                                     July using the latest data from 451 Research’s database and now forecast 217GW of
                                                                                                                     global data center supply by 2030 (v. 168GW prior from our last update in February
                                                                                                                     2026), representing 116GW of additional capacity from 101GW in 2025 (restated higher
                                                                                                                     from 74GW prior). This addition of 116GW would represent $6 tn of capex, assuming a
                                                                                                                     $50B/GW rule-of-thumb while acknowledging that IT infrastructure costs continue to
                                                                                                                     see upward pressure. GS analysts’ see this capex well-covered by current consensus
                                                                                                                     expectations for hyperscale capex (~$1 tn/year on average), which does not include

                                                                                                                     SpaceX AI capex, private company capex, or datacenter platform capex. Further, GS
                                                                                                                     analysts become more positive on the neocloud segment following several large data
                                                                                                                     center lease announcements in the last 3 months including by TeraWulf (Anthropic),
                                                                                                                     Hut8 (undisclosed IG tenant), and others.

                                                                                                                     Are AI infrastructure projects getting delayed? Former Microsoft executive William
                                                                                                                     Fong clarified that recent reports of Microsoft canceling data center MOUs with former
                                                                                                                     bitcoin-mining neoclouds did not signal softening demand for AI training. Instead, these
                                                                                                                     cancellations were driven strictly by the sites’ inability to secure guaranteed power grids
                                                                                                                     and meet the intense energy requirements of modern AI clusters.

                                                                                                                     Are clouds overbuilding? Former Microsoft executive William Fong does not see AI
                                                                                                                     infrastructure at risk of being overbuilt, largely due to the massive demand tailwinds
                                                                                                                     from agentic AI. While inference is shifting toward a hybrid model across the cloud,
                                                                                                                     on-premises, and the edge, there remains a robust demand for net-new AI training
                                                                                                                     infrastructure, especially since the vast majority of the world’s data (of which the
                                                                                                                     internet represents only ~1/3) resides untrained within enterprise systems.

                                                  What is the role of storage appliances and data platforms in AI training and inference?

                                                                                                                                               Background: Following elevated expectations for traditional servers (in addition to
                                                                                                                                               accelerated compute & data center networking), investors are looking to understand
                                                                                                                                               which other enterprise infrastructure hardware categories could also benefit from
                                                                                                                                               agentic AI, such as enterprise storage. Enterprise storage has not seen the same TAM
                                                                                                                                               expansion to date as compute and networking; 650 Group forecasts the external storage
                                                                                                                                               market to grow at a +6% 5-yr CAGR from 2025-30, v. +13% for CPU servers, +45% for AI
                                                                                                                                               servers, and +31% for data center switching.

                                                                                                                                               Does agentic AI drive a need for incremental enterprise storage capacity? In July, we
                                                                                                                                               hosted NTAP’s Chief Product Officer Syam Nair for a deep-dive on AI’s impact on the
                                                                                                                                               storage market. While agentic adoption at the enterprise is still in the early phases,
                                                                                                                                               NTAP management believes that the transition from basic generative AI to autonomous,
                                                                                                                                               multi-step AI agents could drive significant new capacity growth for inferencing. As


                                                  12 August 2026                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               8
                                                  Goldman Sachs                                                                Americas Technology: Hardware

                                                                   management highlighted, AI agents must reason across complex workflows using real
                                                                   enterprise data, which today remains highly siloed, duplicated, and fragmented across
                                                                   different formats (block, file, object) and environments (on-premises and cloud). To
                                                                   prevent these silos from stalling AI initiatives, enterprises require a uniform,
                                                                   disaggregated data platform that provides a single source of truth with built-in
                                                                   governance and security. NetApp addresses this critical bottleneck through its
                                                                   differentiated ONTAP platform (unified management layer across hybrid multicloud
                                                                   environments) and the newly launched AI Data Engine (AIDE). Co-engineered with
                                                                   NVIDIA, AIDE is a secure, unified AI data platform stack that acts as the context memory
                                                                   layer for agentic AI. It automatically creates and continuously updates a global metadata
                                                                   catalog, enabling in-place semantic search, real-time change synchronization, and
                                                                   policy-driven guardrails without requiring excessive data copying.

                                                                   How do legacy storage vendors address neocloud storage/data platform needs? To
                                                                   maximize performance in AI training and inference workloads, neoclouds typically
                                                                   leverage parallel file systems from companies like WEKA (GPUaaS partnership with
                                                                   Nebius) or VAST ($1+ bn commercial partnership with Coreweave). In training, these
                                                                   parallel file systems ensure that data can be quickly accessed by GPUs without




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                   bottlenecks. In inference, high performance storage is needed to make sure that models
                                                                   can be loaded rapidly. While traditional enterprise storage solution providers have not
                                                                   meaningfully participated in neocloud storage needs yet, we are encouraged by the
                                                                   investments being made to expand their portfolios to address the need for more
                                                                   high-performance, disaggregated storage. Specifically, Dell’s new parallel file system is
                                                                   Project Lightning, while NTAP’s recently introduced AFX platform to disaggregated
                                                                   storage architectures. While it’s still early days since these platforms have been
                                                                   introduced, we view the neocloud storage TAM opportunity as incremental to DELL,
                                                                   HPE, and NTAP’s current enterprise-focused exposure.

                                                                   How are vendors navigating higher NAND costs? Gartner is projecting a 200%+ YoY
                                                                   increase in NAND flash prices in 2026 due to unprecedented AI demand. We expect
                                                                   DELL & NTAP should be well-positioned to navigate this volatile pricing environment
                                                                   because of the diversity of its storage portfolio, which spans premium performance




                                                                                                                                                                455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                   flash, high-density capacity flash, and hybrid flash/HDD arrays. Through these expansive
                                                                   portfolios, vendors are able to provide a tiered offering to its customers, moving cold or
                                                                   infrequently accessed data to lower-cost capacity tiers or cloud storage. We are also
                                                                   positive on as-a-service consumption models at NTAP (Keystone) and HPE (Greenlake)
                                                                   as alternate ways to help end customers defray costs as they pay only for what they
                                                                   consume and avoid pre-purchasing expensive flash during peak pricing cycles.

                                                                   Open questions we have on enterprise storage: (1) How much of a margin uplift could

                                                                   there to industry storage estimates in the medium term if agentic AI is more broadly
                                                                   adopted? (3) Have vendors signed LTA’s with NAND/HDD suppliers to secure supply, and
                                                                   if so, at what price? (4) How might incremental industry memory capacity coming on-line
                                                                   from global vendors like YMTX change the supply environment?

                                                  How are PC vendors navigating supply constraints?

                                                                                                                                   Background: Industry PC shipments remain under pressure driven by ongoing
                                                                                                                                   component shortages (memory, storage), as well as a challenging macro backdrop. IDC
                                                                                                                                   estimates that 2Q26 PC shipments (excluding workstations) of 67.0 mn were down -4%
                                                                                                                                   year-over-year, marking the first decline after 9 sequential quarters of positive growth.
                                                                                                                                   In order to navigate lower volumes and higher input costs, PC vendors are raising prices
                                                                                                                                   and re-orienting their portfolios towards higher margin categories like premium
                                                                                                                                   consumer (AI PCs), commercial, and peripherals. PC ASPs reached $1,049 in the quarter,
                                                                                                                                   the highest average selling price in 24 years, up +19% year-over-year.

                                                  Will US hardware OEMs benefit from tariff refunds?




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                                                                      Background: On April 20, US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) launched an online
                                                                                                      portal (called Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries, or CAPE) for
                                                                                                      companies to request refunds from the federal government IEEPA tariffs, which were
                                                                                                      invalidated in February 2026. The tariff refund process will be rolled out across several
                                                                                                      phases, with Phase 2 launching on June 29, 2026. Multinational companies that are
                                                                                                      importers of record and have paid IEEPA tariffs can seek refunds, with refunds going
                                                                                                      directly to the importer of record (not the underlying consumer). The CBP has provided
                                                                                                      guidance that valid IEEPA refunds will generally be issued within 60 - 90 days following
                                                                                                      acceptance of the CAPE Declaration.

                                                                                                      Are tariff refunds contemplated in current outlook? No. Across all four hardware
                                                                                                      stocks, guidance for C2Q26 (three months ending July 31, 2026) and C2026 guidance




                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                                                      did not contemplate tariff refunds. Assuming that each vendor applied & received
                                                                                                      approval for refunds within the first month post the launch of Phase 2 (which we
                                                                                                      understand as encompassing the tariff situations of complex multi-national hardware
                                                                                                      companies), vendors should expect refund to be issues by October 2026 (C3Q26 for
                                                                                                      hardware vendors represents three months ending October 31, 2026).

                                                                                                      How could tariff refunds impact EPS? We expect tariff refunds to have the biggest
                                                                                                      impact on HPQ, which paid ~$500 mn in tariffs and tariff-related expenses in F2025
                                                                                                      largely related to its Print business, per the company. As a reminder, semiconductors
                                                                                                      and select consumer electronics, which encompasses the vast majority of products sold
                                                                                                      by DELL, HPE, and NTAP (and HPQ PCs), were exempt from incremental tariffs during
                                                                                                      the ongoing section 232 investigation.

                                                                                                      While EPS impact will be ultimately be determined by the size of refund, accounting
                                                                                                      treatments, and timing of CBP processing, we estimate that HPQ could see up to a $0.46
                                                                                                      uplift (+15% v. midpoint of guidance) to its full-year EPS outlook if 100% of its tariff and
                                                                                                      tariff-related expenses were re-couped. That said, the CBP is only refunding tariffs paid,
                                                                                                      and vendors are not likely to receive any recompensation for expenses related to the
                                                                                                      re-working of supply chains as a result of tariffs. See analysis below for more details.

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile
                                                  indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
