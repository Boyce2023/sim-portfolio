                                                                  safe distribution of electricity, reducing high transmission voltages to levels suitable for
                                                                  industrial and residential end-uses. We note that there are higher efficient transformers,
                                                                  which are immersed in oil for cooling and insulating internal components. However, over
                                                                  time, technology has advanced, deployments of renewables has increased, and it has
                                                                  created a need for higher powered transformers to allow for continued technological
                                                                  adoptions, particularly in AIDCs.

                                                                  How are solid-state transformers different? A SST, also referred to as a power
                                                                  electronic transformer or “smart” transformer, is an advanced power conversion system
                                                                  that uses high-power semiconductor devices, a compact high-frequency transformer,
                                                                  and sophisticated control circuits to manage and convert electricity. So whereas a
                                                                  traditional transformer acts as a passive device, SSTs offers more active power
                                                                  management. We highlight that SSTs replace the heavy iron cores and copper coils with
                                                                  high-frequency power electronics, leveraging more advanced silicon carbide (SiC) and
                                                                  gallium nitride (GaN) technologies, that allow for greater power efficiency levels while
                                                                  also being more compact. In particular, SSTs can be as much as 50% smaller and lighter,
                                                                  while improving power quality, enabling bidirectionality, and active power management.

                                                                  Advantages and disadvantages of SSTs. SSTs are viewed as critical enablers for future
                                                                  data center power architectures, especially as the power density needs increase, but
                                                                  remain at an early stage of development/deployment. Importantly, as power systems are
                                                                  increasing in complexity, there is a growing focus on leveraging equipment with more
                                                                  advanced controls, better efficiencies, and greater compatibility with renewable energy,
                                                                  rather than simply increasing or decreasing voltage levels. While SSTs maintain similar
                                                                  efficiency levels as traditional transformers (~98%-99%+) under normal conditions, the
                                                                  difference is when there are variable or partial load conditions, which is where SSTs
                                                                  outperform. Additionally, given the compact size and high efficiency, SSTs are ideally
                                                                  suited in applications where space is limited and power demand is high, such as in data
                                                                  centers. A main drawback of SSTs is the higher initial cost as compared to traditional
                                                                  transformers, and based on market data, we estimate SSTs can be ~5x-10x the cost of a
                                                                  traditional transformer. However, as companies continue to advance capabilities around




                                                                                                                                                                 455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                  SSTs and production capacity increases, this can help to reduce costs to make them
                                                                  more affordable. Additionally, when assessing the Total Cost of Ownership, SSTs remain
                                                                  competitive in markets where direct-current (DC) integration capabilities are prioritized,
                                                                  such as data centers.

                                                                                     Why do inverter companies have tech entitlement? What is the importance of
                                                                                     inverters in SST? Inverters are a critical component today for data center applications
                                                                                     by converting AC power from the grid to DC, back to AC, then DC at the server level.
                                                                                     Inverters are also able to convert DC power provided by renewable energy sources to
                                                                                     AC, and then back to DC. Within this conversion process, inverters sit within the
                                                                                     Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS), which are connected to battery backups in case
                                                                                     there are grid outages. However, given the multiple power conversions, this causes
                                                                                     energy to be wasted. Similarly, inverters are also critical components in SSTs that allow
                                                                                     for active power management, rather than the passive devices within traditional




                                                                                                                                                                                         455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                                     transformers. In particular, the inverter can help to adjust the output voltage, frequency,
                                                                                     and help to manage the variable output when using renewable energy sources like solar
                                                                                     and wind. Inverters also can enable bidirectionality capabilities, while also allowing for a
                                                                                     seamless DC-DC integration.

                                                                                     What is the roadmap? GAN vs SiC vs others? SSTs are pursuing Wide Bandgap (WBG)
                                                                                     semiconductor technologies such as gallium nitride (GAN) as well as Silicon Carbide (SiC)
                                                                                     to achieve higher power density, efficiency, and switching frequencies. These
                                                                                     technologies allow for switching up to 10 times faster, which reduces cooling needs and
                                                                                     also allows for bidrectionality. We note that SiC technologies are primarily being pursued
                                                                                     for high power and high-voltage applications, such as 800V-1,200V+ due to their strong
                                                                                     thermal conductivity. Conversely, GAN technologies are being pursued for lower power
                                                                                     and high frequency scenarios due its strong electron mobility capabilities. We note that
                                                                                     GAN is typically used for voltages up to 650V, which is ideal for data centers today, and is
                                                                                     evolving to be used in higher voltages, which could be applied to future data centers.

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a




For the exclusive use of JTWANG@BOYUCAPITAL.COM
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile
                                                  indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns
                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
                                                  For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
