                                                  PM Summary: Initiate at Buy with a $18 12-month PT

                                                                        We believe STDN provides several competitive advantages across the nuclear sector,
                                                                        including a market leading position, revenue and EBITDA generation ahead of peers, and
                                                                        a capital light business model. As a result, we initiate with a Buy rating based on three
                                                                        key factors:

                                                                        n   Significant growth opportunity levered to nuclear expansion. STDN remains
                                                                            exposed to the secular acceleration of power demand growth, which is providing a
                                                                            unique opportunity for the deployment of SMRs. At the same time, we are seeing
                                                                            increased momentum for SMRs advancing towards operational deployments,
                                                                            especially those aimed at government or military applications that provide reduced
                                                                            licensing and fuel availability concerns. Longer-term, we forecast SMR deployments
                                                                            growing to ~15 GW of cumulative capacity by 2035 up from basically zero today,
                                                                            providing strong fuel needs, which STDN is well positioned to execute upon.
                                                                        n   Upstream nuclear fuel supply chain is relatively consolidated and TRISO fuel

                                                  Thesis 1: Significant growth opportunity levered to growth in nuclear

                                                                         Power demand growth is accelerating. Global power demand is expected to continue
                                                                         growing through the end of the decade, driven by a combination of data center
                                                                         expansion, electrification, industrial reshoring and broader economic growth. As
                                                                         highlighted in a recent report from our Utilities team, U.S. power demand is expected to
                                                                         grow at a 3.5% CAGR through 2030 (here), with increasing data center load representing
                                                                         the primary driver of the upward revision. Data centers alone are expected to account
                                                                         for ~14% of total U.S. power demand by 2030, versus ~5% in 2024. Reflecting this trend,
                                                                         we estimate U.S. power demand will increase from ~4,000 TWh in 2020 to more than
                                                                         5,200 TWh by 2030, representing a ~2.8% CAGR. Against this backdrop, demand for
                                                                         reliable baseload generation is increasing, creating a favorable environment for nuclear
                                                                         power growth.

                                                                   Microreactors are gaining momentum through government support and criticality
                                                                   milestones. Advanced reactors represent the next step beyond SMRs, compact,
                                                                   factory-fabricated units typically under 20MW that can be transported by truck or rail
                                                                   and deployed at remote or constrained sites where conventional nuclear plants or grid
                                                                   connections are impractical. Their growing appeal stems from the combination of




                                                                                                                                                                                       455849510b1242a59217c821d3d1e367
                                                                   reliable low-carbon baseload power, minimal on-site construction, lower capital
                                                                   intensity, and flexibility to serve off-grid mines, data centers, and military bases.
                                                                   Momentum is being accelerated by a suite of government programs, including DOE’s
                                                                   Microreactor program and MARVEL demonstration, the DoD’s Project Pele, the U.S.
                                                                   Army’s Janus program, and the Defense Innovation Unit’s ANPI initiative. These efforts
                                                                   are focused on testing, fuel qualification, and early deployment across both civilian and
                                                                   defense applications. A tangible proof point came last month, when four advanced
                                                                   reactors achieved first criticality under the DOE reactor pilot program: Antares Nuclear’s
                                                                   Mark-0, Valar Atomics’ Ward 250, Deployable Energy’s Unity reactor, and Aalo Atomics’s
                                                                   critical test reactor. Collectively, these milestones, supported by rising private
                                                                   investment and licensing activity, indicate that microreactors are advancing from pure
                                                                   R&D into real-world demonstration, positioning them as a meaningful future contributor
                                                                   to nuclear capacity growth.

                                                                     What is TRISO fuel?

                                                                     Tri-structural Isotropic (TRISO) fuel is an advanced nuclear fuel designed for
                                                                     high-temperature. Each particle is a tiny, self-contained fuel unit where a
                                                                     uranium-based kernel is surrounded by a porous carbon buffer layer and three
                                                                     protective coatings — inner pyrolytic carbon, silicon carbide and outer pyrolytic carbon.
                                                                     The silicon-carbide layer is the primary structural barrier and acts as a miniature
                                                                     pressure vessel, while the surrounding layers help manage fission-gas pressure, fuel
                                                                     swelling, and fission-product retention.

                                                                     TRISO particles are then embedded in graphite-based fuel forms tailored to the reactor
                                                                     design. These typically include graphite pebbles, roughly 60mm in diameter, or
                                                                     cylindrical compacts loaded into prismatic graphite blocks. Many advanced reactor
                                                                     designs use HALEU-based TRISO fuel; HALEU contains more than 5% and less than 20%
                                                                     uranium-235, versus up to 5% for conventional LEU fuel.

                                                                                     How is TRISO fuel made?

                                                                                     TRISO fuel manufacturing is a multi-step precision process. Uranium is first formed into
                                                                                     spherical UO₂ or UCO kernels. The kernels are then fluidized in a coating furnace and
                                                                                     sequentially coated by chemical vapour deposition with a porous carbon buffer, inner
                                                                                     pyrocarbon, silicon carbide and outer pyrocarbon. Finished particles are overcoated
                                                                                     with graphite to prevent particle-to-particle damage, then pressed and heat-treated
                                                                                     into either spherical pebbles for pebble bed reactors or cylindrical compacts loaded into
                                                                                     prismatic graphite blocks; some advanced designs instead embed TRISO in a
                                                                                     silicon-carbide matrix, known as Fully Ceramic Microencapsulated (FCM) fuel.
                                                                                     Commercially, the key challenge of TRISO fuel manufacturing is achieving consistent
                                                                                     coating thickness, density, sphericity and low defect rates at scale, as small
                                                                                     manufacturing deviations can directly affect fission-product retention and fuel
                                                                                     qualification.

                                                                                     Why is TRISO important?

                                                                                     TRISO is important because its layered ceramic-and-carbon structure is designed to
                                                                                     tolerate high temperatures, irradiation, oxidation and corrosion better than conventional
                                                                                     fuel forms. This implies that it is a safer form of nuclear fuel. The multiple coating layers
                                                                                     help retain fission products and manage fuel-kernel swelling and fission-gas pressure
                                                                                     under demanding operating conditions. These attributes translate into three principal
                                                                                     advantages:

                                                                                     n    Higher temperature. DOE testing held irradiated UCO-TRISO fuel at 1,600–1,800°C
                                                                                          for approximately 300 hours. At 1,600°C, the combined UCO-TRISO failure fraction
                                                                                          was approximately 9x below the reactor design limit. This supports fission-product
                                                                                          retention during severe heat-up events and contributes to passive HTGR safety
                                                                                          cases. Overall reactor safety also depends on the helium coolant, graphite core, low
                                                                                          power density and passive heat-removal characteristics.
                                                                                     n    Built-in safety. Each particle also functions as a miniature containment system, with
                                                                                          multiple protective layers designed to retain radioactive materials within the fuel.


                                                  10 August 2026                                                                                                               22
                                                  Goldman Sachs                                                                     Standard Nuclear Inc. (STDN)

                                                                       Even if an individual particle is damaged, the surrounding graphite provides an
                                                                       additional barrier that helps limit the release of material into the coolant. This
                                                                       layered protection supports passive-safety reactor designs, particularly for
                                                                       applications such as remote power and industrial heat.
                                                                   n   Higher efficiency. The coating system also supports extended irradiation and higher
                                                                       burnup rates. UCO-TRISO is considered for high-burnup applications of
                                                                       approximately 10-20% Fissions per Initial Metal Atom (FIMA), versus lower burnup
                                                                       for conventional light-water-reactor fuel. Many advanced TRISO reactor designs use
                                                                       HALEU as opposed to LEU fuel, which can enhance reactor performance.

                                                                   Historical Usage of TRISO Fuel

                                                                   TRISO is an established coated-particle fuel technology with commercial operating
                                                                   experience in high-temperature gas-cooled reactor configurations: prismatic-block
                                                                   reactors and pebble-bed reactors. The direct commercial reference base remains
                                                                   limited, however, with two principal operating examples:

                                                                   n   Fort St. Vrain Generating Station — Colorado, U.S.: A 330 MWe helium-cooled,
