                                                                   Raised 2026 guidance and 2027/2028 FCF/share outlook supported by Cornerstone
                                                                   acquisition and strong cash flow conversion. Talen raised its 2026 EBITDA guidance to
                                                                   $2,025-$2,225 mn and FCF to $1,200-$1,350mn, mainly driven by the closing of the
                                                                   Cornerstone acquisition as well as the full ramp of Freedom and Guernsey, offsetting the
                                                                   divestiture of Talen’s minority interest in the Keystone plant. Furthermore, the upward
                                                                   revision in the 2027 and 2028 FCF/share outlooks to $34 and $40 on a base case, and to
                                                                   $37 and $48 when accounting for buybacks, fully layers in the benefits of Talen’s
                                                                   ongoing share repurchase program and is also supported by forward curve movements.
                                                                   Given Talen is only 30% hedged in 2028 compared to 70% in 2027, its highly open
                                                                   position allows it to capture significant upside from strengthening PJM market
                                                                   fundamentals.

                                                                   PPL and AD Hub power price discount to parity opportunity. The PPL and AD Hub
                                                                   power price discounts to the West Hub recently widened to approximately $20/MWh
                                                                   and $12/MWh from their historical average of ~$9/MWh and ~$5/MWh, respectively.
                                                                   Management explained that this discount is mostly driven by transmission outages and
                                                                   upgrades in northern PJM, which is expected to compress as upgrades are completed
                                                                   through 2027-2028 and as rapid load growth materializes directly within the PPL zone.

                                                                   According to management, every $1 improvement in the zonal basis would translate into
                                                                   a $1 increase in FCF/share across the entire portfolio, meaning that if the discount closes
                                                                   to parity or even back to historical levels that would represent a significant FCF/share
                                                                   tailwind relative to the company’s current plan (which holds current basis), and our
                                                                   estimates.

                                                                   Regulatory clarity and contracting dynamics. Management views the recent PJM RBP
                                                                   filing to the FERC as a positive milestone that addresses market uncertainty by
                                                                   establishing rule clarity. While they said that Talen is open to participating in central
                                                                   procurement if it clears at high, capacity-like returns, bilateral contracting remains the
                                                                   company’s preferred path. While some market observers are focused on immediate fall
                                                                   timelines, management views this as a multi-year investment cycle, expecting
                                                                   hyperscalers to continue executing deals in the medium to long term. Management
                                                                   believes that this regulatory clarity, combined with the lack of resource adequacy issues

                                                                   PPA strategy and development pipeline positioned for front-of-the-meter data
                                                                   center solutions. Talen is progressing a 4 GW site development pipeline of powered
                                                                   land and data center contracting options, alongside +2 GW of newbuild capacity

                                                  GS Factor Profile
                                                  The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks)
                                                  and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth,
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The
                                                  normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
                                                  vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
                                                  Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a
                                                  higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial
                                                  stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B,
                                                  price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile

                                                  percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
                                                  Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs
                                                  for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
                                                  For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

                                                  M&A Rank

                                                  companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2
                                                  representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard
                                                  departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not
